# Fallback document outlines

Use these only when the firm has no template of its own. A firm's existing engagement letter
has usually been through a professional body or an insurer. Yours has not. Always ask first.

## Engagement letter

1. Parties. Full legal names, company numbers, registered addresses.
2. Scope. Each service listed separately. What is included, and a short "not included" list.
3. Period. The first period covered, and how it renews.
4. Client responsibilities. Records, deadlines, accuracy of information supplied.
5. Firm responsibilities. What you will do, and by when.
6. Fees. Basis, frequency, what triggers a repricing.
7. Limitation of liability. From the firm's professional body wording, never drafted here.
8. Termination. Notice on both sides, what happens to records.
9. Signature block. Named signatory and role on both sides.

The "not included" list is the clause that prevents most scope arguments. Write it explicitly.

## Information request

Order by where the client keeps things, not by where they sit in the accounts. A client
gathers everything from their bank in one go, then everything from their email.

1. Bank. Statements for every account, for the full period. Ask how many accounts exist.
2. Sales. Invoices raised, or platform access.
3. Purchases. Supplier invoices and receipts.
4. Payroll. Payslips, pension, any changes in the period.
5. Loans and finance. Agreements and statements.
6. Assets. Anything bought or sold over the capitalisation threshold.
7. Other. Anything unusual that happened in the period.

Against each line put the format you want and the deadline.
